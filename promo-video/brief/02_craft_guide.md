# 02 — Craft Guide: What Makes a Premium 35–50 s Launch Film

Author: Fable 5.1 (planning / research pass). For: Opus 5.5 (production / aesthetics pass) and reviewers.
Scope: a brand/launch film for a curated, premium social app (working-title brand proposed in 01/03 briefs). Copy in English, short lines, swappable to Chinese.

Sourcing note: WebSearch was available but returned almost nothing useful on the specific films (only brand-identity write-ups and subtitle reading-speed standards). The film analyses below (Apple, Arc, Threads/Bluesky, Linear, Notion, Teenage Engineering, Nothing, Pinterest/Instagram, luxury fashion, Xiaohongshu) are from my own knowledge and should be treated as informed craft judgement, not citations. Reading-speed numbers in §2 are from published subtitle standards (BBC 160–180 wpm ≈ 14–16 CPS; Netflix ≤17–20 CPS, min cue ~5/6 s, ≤42 chars/line).

---

## 0. What the references actually do (reverse-engineered)

| Reference | The one thing to steal | The trap to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Apple product films | One sentence per shot; product fills frame; black or pure white with nothing else; every cut lands on a musical hit. | Copying the hardware-porn macro shots when you have a software product. |
| Arc browser launch (2023) | Warm paper tones, serif + grotesk pairing, hand-made feel, confident silence, product shown in real use. Felt "made by people," not a template. | Over-whimsy; too many stickers/doodles reads cheap outside their brand. |
| Threads / Bluesky launch spots | Speed to the point: product in <3 s, clear "what it is" line, friendly, no over-explaining. | Cheap stock-smiling-people montage. |
| Linear brand film | Dark field, ultra-fine type, light as a material (glows, edge highlights), UI treated like an object in a studio. Slow push-ins. Minimal palette. | Dark + glow becomes "crypto" if the glow is saturated and the type is bold. |
| Notion brand films | Off-white "paper" world, large type, illustrated warmth, a single human truth as the hook. | Clip-art-level illustration density. |
| Teenage Engineering | Deadpan, mono/grotesk, grid, product-as-instrument, no music swell, almost documentary. | Being so dry it reads as placeholder. |
| Nothing phone | Industrial, dot-matrix type, monochrome with a single accent, rhythmic hard cuts on beats, high-contrast macro. | Edgy-for-edgy; glitch effects. |
| Pinterest / Instagram brand spots | Real creator content as the hero; the UI is a frame, not the star. Fast grid-to-single transitions. | Faux UGC with influencer-ad lighting. |
| Luxury fashion digital films (Loewe, Bottega, Jacquemus) | Negative space, long holds, slow dolly, tactile texture, grain, serif at small sizes, logo as the last whisper. | Over-polished CGI sheen; cheap lens flares. |
| Xiaohongshu brand films | Warmth, "life is beautiful" earnestness, real moments, soft daylight, intimate scale; a product that feels like a friend. | Saccharine; over-saturated reds. |

**Synthesis for our film:** a *luxury-fashion-film discipline* (negative space, holds, grain, serif) carrying a *Linear/Apple-grade product treatment* (UI as a lit object, one line per shot) with *Pinterest/XHS warmth* (real-looking creator work as the hero). Dark or paper is a decision to make once and keep — see §5.

## 1. Structural beats of a 35–50 s launch film

Target: **42 s** (fits 30-s cut-down and 60-s extension later). Timings below are for 42 s; scale proportionally.

| # | Beat | Duration | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook (the itch)** | 0–4 s | One image + one line that names the feeling the viewer already has. No logo. Silence or sub-bass only. E.g. an endless, noisy feed as a texture; line: "Scroll. Scroll. Scroll." or "Your feed is loud." | The first 2 s decide if the viewer stays; a question/truth beats a claim. |
| 2 | **Tension / problem** | 4–9 s | 2–3 quick cuts that dramatize the problem (noise, sameness, clutter). Pace accelerates. Audio: rising texture / ticking. | Create the pressure the product will release. Keep it short — negative framing >6 s makes the brand feel sour. |
| 3 | **Turn / breath** | 9–11 s | Hard cut to emptiness. One beat of silence. A single word: "Enough." / "Quiet." / "Curated." | The contrast is the reveal; silence is the most expensive sound in the film. |
| 4 | **Reveal** | 11–17 s | The product appears as one beautiful object — one piece of content filling the frame, then the frame pulls back to show the app. Brand name may appear small here or be held for the end. Music enters properly (first full chord / downbeat). | Establish "what it is" by *showing*, not explaining. |
| 5 | **Proof points** | 17–26 s | 2–3 shots, each one line: "Every post, hand-picked." / "No junk. No noise." / "Only work that deserves your eyes." Continuous transform between them (grid → single → grid). | Why it's different; the curation promise. |
| 6 | **Feature run (creator tools)** | 26–35 s | 3–4 fast but *continuous* UI moments: editing/color tools, layout/publish, analytics/monetization. One verb per line: "Make." "Shape." "Earn." | Prove it's for creators too; this is where tempo peaks. |
| 7 | **Human beat** | 35–38 s | One real-feeling shot of a creator's work or hands; line: "Made for the people who make things." | Reconnect feature run to feeling before the logo. |
| 8 | **Logo / CTA** | 38–42 s | Cut to clean field; wordmark rises in; one line beneath ("Now on iOS" / "Join the first thousand"). Single thump/whoosh, then tail silence 0.8–1.2 s. | A logo that lands on a hit is remembered; the tail of silence makes it feel finished. |

Rules of thumb:
- Product visible by **≤ 12 s**; brand name by **≤ 20 s** (small) and again at the end (big).
- Never more than **3 consecutive negative shots**.
- Peak tempo at ~70–80 % of runtime (feature run), then decelerate into logo.
- Total on-screen lines: **8–12** for 42 s. Over 14 is a slideshow.

## 2. Pacing rules

**Shot lengths**
- Hook hold: 2.5–4 s. Tension cuts: 0.6–1.2 s. Reveal hold: 3–5 s. Proof shots: 2–3 s each. Feature run: 1.5–2.2 s each. Human beat: 2.5–3 s. Logo: 3–4 s + silence tail.
- Shortest permissible cut in a premium film: **~12 frames (0.5 s)** and only in the tension beat. Below that reads as a cheap stinger.
- Longest hold: ~5 s, and only if something inside the frame is moving (slow push, light shift, type settling).

**Cut vs. continuous transform**
- **Cut** when: changing *world* (problem → product), landing a musical hit, or giving a line its own breath.
- **Continuously transform** (morph, camera move, element persists across) when: showing the *same object in a new state* — grid → single post → editing tool → published post. This is what creates the "one continuous shot" feel and separates it from a template.
- Ratio for this film: roughly **60 % transforms / 40 % cuts**; cuts cluster in beats 2, 3 and 8.
- Each transform should have a *carrier*: one element that survives the transition (a photo, a card, a cursor, a colour field). Without a carrier the transform is just a wipe.

**On-screen copy**
- One idea per line; one line per shot (two lines max, and only in the reveal or logo).
- **Max 6 words per line**; headline lines ideally 2–4. Max ~32 characters so the Chinese swap (usually shorter) and a long German/Spanish one both fit.
- Minimum dwell: **1.0 s for ≤3 words**, then add **~0.35 s per extra word** (≈ 15–17 CPS incl. recognition time). Practical table:
  - 1–2 words: 1.0–1.4 s
  - 3–4 words: 1.5–2.0 s
  - 5–6 words: 2.1–2.8 s
  - Add 0.3 s if the line enters with an animation (the viewer can't read during the in).
- Lines never overlap each other on screen. New line = old line already gone or ≥ 90 % out.
- Never put a line on top of a busy moving image; park copy in negative space or dim the image 20–30 %.

## 3. Typography rules for motion

**Pairing**
- Display: a high-contrast **serif** (e.g. editorial Didone-ish or a modern transitional — think GT Super, Editorial New, Canela, Reckless). Carries emotion lines ("Your feed is loud.").
- Functional: a neutral **grotesk** (Inter, Söhne, Suisse Int'l, Neue Haas Grotesk, Geist). Carries UI, captions, CTA, feature verbs.
- Use **exactly two families**. A third is allowed only if it's a mono for a tiny "v1.0 / 2026" label.

**Scale & hierarchy**
- Display lines: 7–11 % of frame height at 1080p (≈ 76–120 px). Only one display line per shot.
- Sub-lines/captions: 1.8–2.4 % of frame height (≈ 20–26 px). Never more than 2 sizes on screen.
- Keep a single baseline grid (e.g. 8 px) and a consistent left margin (e.g. 8–10 % of width) so lines across shots feel like one layout.

**Tracking / leading / length**
- Serif display: tracking **−1 % to −2.5 %**, leading 0.95–1.05.
- Grotesk small: tracking **+2 % to +6 %** for captions and all-caps labels; all-caps only for ≤3-word labels.
- Measure: ≤ 32 chars/line, ≤ 2 lines.
- Weights: display Regular/Light; grotesk Medium for UI. Bold display reads as retail.

**Animating type tastefully**
- **Masked rise:** text rises 100–120 % of cap-height from behind a mask, 500–700 ms, expo-out. The default, especially for the serif.
- **Blur-in + 2–4 % scale settle:** from blur 8–12 px / opacity 0 / scale 1.03 → clear, 450–650 ms, quint-out. Good for the reveal line and logo.
- **Tracking settle:** letter-spacing from +6 % → 0 over 700–900 ms while fading in. Only for short display lines; never for paragraphs.
- **Word-by-word stagger:** 40–70 ms per word, same easing. Never letter-by-letter typewriter (unless deliberately mono/TE-style and only once).
- **Exit:** faster than the entry (60–70 % of in-duration), usually fade + 4–8 px drift *in the direction of the next shot's motion*, or hard cut on a beat.
- Type should never bounce, elastic-overshoot, or spin. Max overshoot: 1–2 %.

**What looks cheap**
- Default sans at bold weight, centered, with drop shadow or stroke.
- Text that slides fully across the frame; "typewriter" with a blinking cursor.
- More than 2 fonts, more than 2 sizes, random alignment per shot.
- Tracking at 0 on small caps; too-tight leading on 2-line serif.
- Glow on text; gradient-filled text; 3D extrude.
- Lines that enter *after* the music hit instead of *on* it.

## 4. Motion principles

**Easing**
- UI / type / layout: **expo-out** (`cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`) or **quint-out** (`cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`). Durations 400–800 ms.
- Cinematic / camera moves: slow symmetrical curves, e.g. `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)` over 2–5 s, or linear-with-tiny-ease for push-ins that span a whole shot.
- Exits: **expo-in** or `cubic-bezier(0.7, 0, 0.84, 0)`, shorter than entries.
- Never linear for anything but texture drift; never ease-in-out "swing" for UI (it feels like PowerPoint).

**Overlap and stagger**
- Nothing starts and stops at the same frame as anything else. Offset secondary elements by 60–120 ms, tertiary by 120–220 ms.
- Stagger lists/grids 30–60 ms per item, capped at ~8 visible items (then group).
- Hold ~15–25 % of each shot with *nothing new entering* so the eye can rest.

**Camera-like moves**
- Permanent slow **push-in** or **drift** on every static plate (1–3 % scale over the shot). Dead-still frames look like slides.
- **Parallax**: 2–3 depth layers (background texture / content cards / type) moving at ratios ≈ 1 : 1.6 : 2.2. Type is the nearest layer.
- Simulated dolly and rack-focus (blur the back layer 2–6 px) when the product is "placed" in a studio.
- Tiny handheld noise (0.2–0.4 px, 0.3–0.6 Hz) on live-action-feeling shots only; none on UI.

**"One continuous shot" feel**
- Maintain a *carrier object* across transitions (§2). Example chain: a photo card → expands to full-bleed hero → shrinks into a grid cell → the grid becomes the app's feed → the feed's top card opens into the editor → the editor's export button becomes the logo mark.
- Keep one consistent *direction of travel* for the film (e.g. everything moves gently up and toward the camera). Reversals only on the "turn" beat.
- Match-cut on shape and position: the next shot's hero element starts where the previous one ended.

**Avoiding the template look**
- No stock transitions (slide, wipe, zoom-blur, light-leak, glitch, shake).
- No elements that "pop" from 0 % scale. Enter from 92–96 % or from a mask.
- Imperfection on purpose: grain, slight asymmetry in layout, a human-set type size, one shot that holds slightly too long.
- Product UI must be the *real* UI (or indistinguishable) — real content density, real thumbnails, real text, not lorem ipsum or grey boxes.
- Delete anything that exists only to "fill" a shot.

## 5. Colour and light

**The one big decision: dark or paper**
- **Deep black / near-black (#0A0A0B – #111)**: luxury, cinema, light as material. Suits "content as art," glowing cards, Linear/Apple feel. Risk: becomes "crypto" or "gaming" if accent is saturated or glow is heavy.
- **Off-white / paper (#F4F1EC – #FAF8F3)**: warmth, editorial, Arc/Notion/XHS feel. Suits "human, curated, magazine." Risk: looks like a stationery brand if type is too dainty.
- **Recommendation for this product:** *mostly dark* (lets curated imagery glow and reads premium-exclusive), with one or two *paper* interludes (the human beat and perhaps the logo) so the film breathes. Decide once; never alternate per shot.

**Accent discipline**
- One accent only, used in ≤ 5 % of frames: the logo mark, a cursor, one underline, one "Earn" number. Everything else is black/white/greys plus the colour that lives inside the curated content itself.
- Accent candidates that avoid Instagram's magenta-orange and Xiaohongshu's red: warm ochre, muted coral, deep oxblood, or an electric lilac (used sparingly). Test against the content library; the accent should never fight the photos.

**Grain, vignette, glass, bloom**
- Film grain: fine, monochrome, 3–6 % intensity, animated at 24 fps (not every frame of 60), slightly stronger in shadows. Grain unifies UI and photo layers.
- Vignette: 8–15 %, soft, barely perceptible; stronger (20 %) only in the hook/tension beats.
- Glass/blur: use *one* glass material (backdrop blur 20–40 px, 6–10 % white, 1 px hairline at 10–15 % white). Frosted panels stacked three deep look like a UI kit.
- Bloom: only from light sources inside the image or the single accent; threshold high, radius large, intensity low (< 10 %). No bloom on text.
- Light as narrative: let a soft key-light drift across the product surface during the reveal (a gradient mask moving 1 px/frame). Light changes read as "camera," static gradients read as "template."

**Imagery**
- Curated content must look *actually* curated: consistent tonal range, no clip-art, no AI-smooth faces. Prefer still photography, fashion, interiors, food, hands making things, type, architecture; mix of warm and cool but graded to one look.
- Grade the entire film in one LUT-like pass: slightly lifted blacks (RGB 8–12), soft highlight roll-off, a hint of warmth in midtones.

## 6. Sound

**Music**
- One track, 42 s, preferably with a clear structure: textural intro (0–9 s), a single accent at the "turn" (≈10 s), downbeat at the reveal (≈11–12 s), build through features, drop-out before the logo, and a final hit.
- Tempo 80–100 BPM feels premium; 120+ feels like a sale. Cut lengths should be whole beats or half-beats of the track.
- Prefer sparse: piano/strings/sub-bass/texture over four-on-the-floor. No vocal hooks (they fight the copy).

**Hits and cuts**
- Every hard cut in beats 4–8 lands **on** a musical accent, or 1–2 frames *after* (never before — the sound should cause the picture).
- Transforms begin on a beat and *resolve* on the next bar; the hold begins on the resolution.
- Subtle UI SFX (soft clicks, paper ticks, 20–40 ms) for 3–5 key interactions in the feature run, mixed 12–18 dB below music.

**Silence**
- Open with near-silence (room tone/sub) for ≥ 1.5 s.
- The "turn" is a full-band drop-out of 0.5–1.0 s.
- The film ends with 0.8–1.2 s of silence after the logo hit. Reviewers should feel the room go quiet.

**The single logo sound**
- One low "thump" (60–90 Hz, short) with an airy high "whoosh/riser" tail (≈ 300–500 ms) that reverses into the thump. Use it *once*. Its onset = the frame the wordmark reaches 90 % of its final position.
- No cheesy "swoosh" on every element.

**Mix**
- Music peaks ≈ −6 dBTP, dialogue/VO (if any) ≈ −12 LUFS short-term, integrated loudness −16 LUFS (web) / −14 LUFS (social). Leave headroom; a crushed mix sounds cheap.

## 7. Premium-vs-cheap checklist (15 tells)

Score each 0/1; ≥ 13 = ship, 10–12 = fix, < 10 = redo.

1. **Product visible by 12 s, logo big at the end** — not logo-first, not explain-first.
2. **≤ 12 on-screen lines, each ≤ 6 words**, each held long enough to read (§2 table).
3. **Two typefaces only** (serif display + grotesk), one display size, one caption size, consistent margins.
4. **Type enters from masks/blur with expo/quint-out; no bounce, no typewriter, no shadow/stroke.**
5. **Nothing is ever perfectly static** — every plate has a push, drift or light move.
6. **Transitions have a carrier object**; no stock wipes/slides/zoom-blur/glitch/light-leaks.
7. **Cuts land on musical hits**; cut lengths are on the beat grid.
8. **Silence used at least twice** (open, turn, or logo tail).
9. **One accent colour, ≤ 5 % of frames**; palette otherwise neutral; one grade across the film.
10. **Grain/vignette/bloom are felt, not seen** (grain ≤ 6 %, bloom < 10 %, no bloom on text).
11. **One glass material, used ≤ 3 times**; no stacked frosted panels.
12. **UI is real**: real content, real density, real strings; no grey boxes, lorem ipsum, or default icons.
13. **Curated imagery actually looks curated** — consistent tonal world, no stock-smile people, no AI-smooth faces.
14. **Overlap/stagger everywhere**: no two elements start/stop on the same frame; rests of 15–25 % per shot.
15. **The logo gets one sound, one move, and a quiet tail**; no end-card clutter (max: wordmark + one line + one store badge).

Bonus red flags (instant "cheap"): emoji on screen, more than one exclamation mark in the whole film, a countdown, "coming soon" in neon, a phone mock-up that spins 360°.

## 8. Quick spec for the production pass

- 1920×1080 (plus 1080×1920 vertical re-frame later), 24 fps, ProRes/HQ master, 42 s + 1 s black tail.
- Safe areas: title-safe 90 %; keep copy in the left 8–10 % margin column or centred on an explicit grid, never both in one film.
- Deliver copy as a separate layer/JSON so EN→ZH swap is a data change, not a re-render. Brand name as a single variable.
- Keep a shot list with: beat, in/out TC, line, carrier object, easing, music cue, SFX.
